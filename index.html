What most estimates do
Cost × 1.20

Add 20% to a $32,000 cost and quote $38,400. Feels like a 20% job. The profit is $6,400 on a $38,400 sale — 16.7%.

What actually keeps 20%
Cost ÷ 0.80

The same $32,000 cost has to be quoted at $40,000 to keep a real 20%. That is $1,600 more on one job — and every job.

Straight answers.

Where is my data?
In your browser, on your device, and nowhere else. There is no server and no account, so there is no copy of your client list anywhere. The real trade-off: clearing your browser data erases it, and it does not sync between your phone and your laptop. Export a backup regularly — the button is on the Settings tab.
Are the price book numbers right for my market?
Probably not exactly, and you should change them. They are representative US ranges meant to get you to a first quote in a minute instead of an hour. Every cost is editable, and the app treats them as your cost, never a sell price.
Is the signature legally binding?
It records acceptance on the device in front of you, which is genuinely useful and what most small jobs run on. It is not a full e-signature service — there is no identity verification, tamper seal, or audit trail. For large contracts or anything you might have to defend, use a dedicated e-sign provider.
Why is it free? What's the catch?
There is no server, so it costs essentially nothing to run — that is a genuine consequence of the architecture, not a loss leader. If a paid tier ever exists it would be for things that actually require a server, like syncing across devices or sending a proposal from a hosted link. What is here today stays free and keeps working offline either way.
Will this replace my accounting or job costing software?
No. This prices work and produces proposals. It does not track actual costs against the estimate, run payroll, invoice, or talk to QuickBooks. Export CSV and hand it to whatever you already use.
Does raising my prices lose me jobs?
Sometimes, yes. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. The honest case for pricing correctly is that a job won at 12% margin can be worse than a job you did not win at all, because it consumes the crew weeks that a properly priced job needed. The app shows you which one you are looking at; the call is still yours.
