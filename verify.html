Check an n-of-1 trial certificate

Paste a certificate below. This page recomputes its p-value from the numbers it carries and tells you whether the result follows from the design. Everything happens in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere, and this file works with the network switched off.

The JSON block from the trial. It contains no diary, no dates and no symptom log — only the per-pair differences needed to reproduce the arithmetic.

Optional, and the half that matters most. If someone handed you a twelve-character code before their trial began, enter it here to confirm this is that trial — same question, same length, same coin tosses. Without it the arithmetic is still checkable, but that the question came first rests on the certificate's own word.

What this can and cannot tell you. It checks that the result follows from the design, that the numbers are internally consistent, and — given the code — that the design matches what was registered before any data existed. It does not tell you who produced the certificate. Nothing here is a signature, and someone determined to fabricate one can invent the numbers and compute the digests. Identity comes from whoever held the registration code: the clinician who watched the trial, or a registry that timestamped it on receipt.

A single trial on one person is evidence about that person. It is not evidence about you.
