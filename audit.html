Find out what your last three jobs really kept.

You send the numbers from three recent jobs — what you quoted, what you paid out, what got added along the way. You get back a one-page report per job, in your own numbers, showing exactly where the money went. Most contractors have never once seen this laid out.

Book an audit

What you get

  1. A report per job: what it actually kept. Revenue against real costs, with your overhead applied. The margin you got, next to the margin you priced for. On most jobs these are not the same number, and the gap has a dollar figure on it.
  2. Every dollar of work that never got billed. The "while you're in there" extras, the verbal change orders, the rot repair nobody wrote up. Itemized. This is usually the number that stings — it is work you remember doing, for free, recently.
  3. Where each job bled past its budget. Overruns by trade, so you know whether the problem is your labor number, your tile sub, or your quote. That tells you what to fix before the next bid repeats it.
  4. The number your next quote has to be. Not "charge more" — the specific price, at your real costs, that hits the margin you need. You can take it or leave it, but you will know it.

What this is not

Not an accounting engagement. The reports are built from the records you provide and say so on their face. Overhead is applied at your stated rate, not measured. It is not tax advice, not a valuation, and it will not tell you anything your own numbers do not contain — the point is that nobody has ever laid your own numbers out this way.
